LIT NES Punch-Out!! (1987) is built around readable tells: opponents flash, raise their fists or make a sound before each attack, and players learn each boxer’s patterns. The NTSC NES draws 60.0988 frames per second, so 0.3 s is 18 frames, 0.2 s is 12 and 0.1 s is 6.

MODEL {{-2,0,3}} as a filter (as in v42/v43): three time taps, oldest × −2, middle × 0, newest × 3. The weights sum to 1, so a steady signal passes unchanged, and it equals now + 2 × (now − then): a forward extrapolation. With OBSERVE’s 6-frame taps a straight wind-up is read 24 frames (0.40 s) ahead; SUB ID uses 2-frame taps.

MODEL Signaling game: the opponent is a real puncher or a feinter. A wind-up is the signal, and Patricia dodges or counters. Getting hit costs L = 2, a wasted dodge c = 1, a landed counter gains G = 1. She should dodge when P(real | tell) > P* = (G + c)/(G + c + L). In equilibrium the opponent feints just often enough to keep her indifferent: a semi-separating equilibrium.

AMBER A feint is the same wind-up until it stops, so no filter can tell them apart early. The SUB ID veto (half lean when OBSERVE peaks but the last 0.1 s is calm) helps only after the feint turns. Human simple visual reaction time is often quoted around 0.2–0.25 s; that figure was not re-browsed for this page.

MODEL Two agents, as Dave set for every world: PATRICIA and OPPONENT play Dodge-or-Counter against Punch-or-Feint (zero-sum, MODEL) over and over by regret matching (Hart & Mas-Colell 2000), each shifting toward the moves it regrets not having played. Their score is 1 − NashConv/4 of their empirical average play (1 = an exact Nash equilibrium), and the I-13 VM re-computes NashConv from the raw averages every time the score improves. The centrepiece is a folded star {9/4} whose fold rises with the score. Three.js r160 from a pinned CDN, devicePixelRatio capped at 2. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom.
