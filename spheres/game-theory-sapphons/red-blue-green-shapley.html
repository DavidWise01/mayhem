MODEL Sapphon notes are read from PATRICIA v43 (nes_agents.js in the NES Agents app): Dave’s words are quoted exactly as he typed them, and every game-theory mapping on this page is my reading, not his.

LIT Lloyd Shapley, “A value for n-person games” (Contributions to the Theory of Games II, 1953, pp. 307–317): average each player’s marginal contribution v(S ∪ i) − v(S) over all orders in which the coalition could form. It is the only rule that is efficient, symmetric, additive and gives null players 0. Shapley shared the 2012 Nobel with Roth.

LIT The core: splits that give every sub-coalition at least what it could get alone. In convex games the Shapley value is always in the core.

MODEL Characteristic function (sliders): v(RED) 1, v(BLUE) 2, v(GREEN) 0.5, v(RED+BLUE) 5, v(RED+GREEN) 2.5, v(BLUE+GREEN) 3.5, v(all) 8, read as how much of a successful turn each team of sapphons produces. PATRICIA’s blend weights are taken as Shapley value / v(all). These numbers are mine, not measured in v43.

MODEL The agents are BLUE and GREEN in an inspection game: BLUE can be careful or hasty, GREEN can check or trust. GREEN checks with probability 1/3 and BLUE is hasty 1/4 of the time in equilibrium.

MODEL Two agents, as Dave set for every world: BLUE and GREEN play the BLUE–GREEN inspection game (MODEL) over and over by regret matching (Hart & Mas-Colell 2000), each shifting toward the moves it regrets not having played. Their score is 1 − NashConv/3 of their empirical average play (1 = an exact Nash equilibrium), and the I-13 VM re-computes NashConv from the raw averages every time the score improves. The centrepiece is a folded star {13/5} whose fold rises with the score. Three.js r160 from a pinned CDN, devicePixelRatio capped at 2. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom.

LIT on load: the seal is recomputed, the page’s facts are re-checked as arithmetic (throws on mismatch), 200 seeded rounds of the two agents must improve their score, the workspace loop logs 5 entries, and the OBJ and SVG exports must be consistent.
