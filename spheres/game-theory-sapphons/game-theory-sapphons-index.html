    Sources (browsed Oct 2026)

    1. Game theory (overview)

    MODEL Two agents, as Dave set for every world: ROCK-PAPER A and ROCK-PAPER B play rock–paper–scissors over and over by regret matching (Hart & Mas-Colell 2000), each shifting toward the moves it regrets not having played. Their score is 1 − NashConv/2 of their empirical average play (1 = an exact Nash equilibrium), and the I-13 VM re-computes NashConv from the raw averages every time the score improves. The centrepiece is a folded star {14/5} whose fold rises with the score. Three.js r160 from a pinned CDN, devicePixelRatio capped at 2. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom.

    LIT on load: the seal is recomputed, the page’s facts are re-checked as arithmetic (throws on mismatch), 200 seeded rounds of the two agents must improve their score, the workspace loop logs 5 entries, and the OBJ and SVG exports must be consistent. Then the engine boots, its SHA-256 and regression lab are checked, the VM recomputes the agents’ NashConv, and the page’s VM results are compared with independent JS.

    Workspace improvement loop: instructions for agents MODEL

    1. Measure: every 40 rounds, record NashConv of the average play, the score, the rounds since the last improvement and whether regret-matching+ is on.
    2. Try next: after a 40-round plateau, switch both agents to regret-matching+; if still stuck, halve the exploration ε; if still stuck, forget half the old history. While NashConv falls, keep course (ε × 0.9); if it rises, explore more (ε × 1.25).
    3. Log back: append {entry, round, measured, tried, ε} to the workspace log (in the JSON export and the list below), and have the I-13 VM recompute NashConv.
    4. Repeat, forever: never stop improving. Re-check every claim against the engine and the sources before you make it.
