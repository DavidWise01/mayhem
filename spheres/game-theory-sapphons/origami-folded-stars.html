Folded Paper Stars · Lucky Stars & Froebel

Tie a paper strip in an overhand knot, flatten it, and the knot is a regular pentagon. Wrap the rest of the strip around it and pinch the edges, and it puffs into a lucky star. Four woven strips make a 16-pointed Froebel star. The I-13 VM computes the pentagon and the fold angles.

LIT What the VM computes here: The VM computes the knot pentagon’s side and area with its own sine series, and the folded star’s tip dihedral from raw vertex coordinates (cross products, dot product, Newton √). JS does the final acos and the puffing animation.

LIT Lucky stars: start with a strip of about 1:13, tie a loose overhand knot, pull it snug and flatten it into a neat pentagon. Fold the short tail onto it, wrap the long tail around the pentagon edge by edge, tuck the end, then hold the pentagon by its edges and press each side in with a fingernail so it puffs into a five-pointed star.

MODEL Geometry of the knot (derived here): the strip runs along a side and the parallel diagonal of the pentagon, which are s·sin 72° apart, so a strip of width w makes a pentagon of side s = w / sin 72° ≈ 1.0515 w. The number of wraps is estimated as (13w − 2s)/s.

LIT Froebel star (Fröbelstern): woven and folded from four identical strips of about 1:25 to 1:30, in about forty steps. It ends with eight flat prongs and eight cone tips. Named after Friedrich Fröbel, founder of the kindergarten, who promoted paper folding, but he probably didn’t invent it. Instructions date to at least the 19th century (an 1885 Dorcas Magazine article), and the German name has been common since the 1960s.

MODEL Two agents, as Dave set for every world: FOLDER A and FOLDER B play two folders choosing valley or mountain creases (pure coordination) over and over by regret matching (Hart & Mas-Colell 2000), each shifting toward the moves it regrets not having played. Their score is 1 − NashConv/2 of their empirical average play (1 = an exact Nash equilibrium), and the I-13 VM re-computes NashConv from the raw averages every time the score improves. The centrepiece is a folded star {5/2} whose fold rises with the score. Three.js r160 from a pinned CDN, devicePixelRatio capped at 2. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom.
