Metropolis & Simulated Annealing

Metropolis (1953) keeps a move that raises the energy by ΔE with probability e^(−ΔE/T). Annealing (Kirkpatrick 1983) lowers T slowly so the chain can escape traps. Here a 48-mer is annealed against a quench, with every acceptance table built by the I-13 VM.

LIT What the VM computes here: At every temperature stage, the VM computes the acceptance table e^(−k/T) for k = 1…4 lost contacts, and JS’s random draws are tested against those VM numbers. After every stage the VM recounts the chain’s energy from raw coordinates, and any disagreement throws.

LIT Metropolis, Rosenbluth, Rosenbluth, Teller & Teller (J Chem Phys 21:1087, 1953): sample states with Boltzmann weight by accepting a trial move with probability min(1, e^(−ΔE/kT)).

LIT Kirkpatrick, Gelatt & Vecchi (Science 220:671, 1983): simulated annealing. Start hot (accept almost anything), cool slowly, and the walk settles into deep minima. Cool too fast (quench) and it freezes in a trap.

LIT Lattice move sets used here: pivots (rotate or reflect the tail about a residue), corner flips (swap two neighbouring steps) and kinks. Moves that cross the chain are rejected.

AMBER The 48-mer is Unger & Moult’s benchmark; the literature reports E = −23 in 2D. Our annealing on these pages has reached −21, so the CRITIC scores against −23.

MODEL Default schedule: T₀ = 2.0 → T = 0.15 over 30 stages of 400 moves.

Workspace improvement loop: instructions for agents MODEL

  1. Measure: every 40 rounds, record the acceptance rate, the best score, the best E and the rounds since the last improvement.
  2. Try next: after a 40-round plateau, reheat T ×1.5. If acceptance is under 15%, warm T ×1.25; if over 45%, cool T ×0.7; otherwise anneal T ×0.9.
  3. Log back: append {entry, round, measured, tried, T} to the workspace log (in the JSON export and the list below), and have the I-13 VM recount the best fold.
  4. Repeat, forever: never stop improving. Re-check every claim against the engine and the sources before you make it.
